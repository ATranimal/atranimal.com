*I'm a top 200 level Super Smash Bros Melee player, and I'm preparing for my modern first RC in October. I got into magic during Tarkir Dragon Storm last year as some of my Melee friends were starting to get into Commander. I'm documenting my process of learning Modern as a player with no knowledge of constructed or any cards before my time.* 

On June 28th, with a very lucky sealed pool containing **Super Hero Civil War** and **Storm, Windrider**, I managed to top 8 my local RCQ of 25 players without dropping a game. I drafted a fairly open BR deck with **Doctor Doom** and **Avengers: Under Siege** to make top 4 and secure a spot in Canada's Modern RC in Ottawa on October 3rd.

For my first year of playing, I've pretty exclusively been playing Draft. I **LOVE** playing draft. I've been obsessed with roguelike deckbuilders since playing Slay the Spire non-stop during Covid, and draft is a perfect marriage of the competitive gameplay I love from fighting games and the risk reward calculation of deck builders. I thought this would be a great opportunity to commit myself to competing in a new game, and giving myself an opportunity to see how much I can learn and improve in a tight deadline. I've always enjoyed picking up new fighting games and e-sports and becoming high ranked in them -- though none of them are as storied and daunting as Magic. I **really** don't know many old cards; For example, when looking through the Tron list the ONLY card that I recognized was Ugin, Eye of the Storms.

So I created a concrete plan to give myself the best shot of performing well at the RC:

1. Proxy and play with the top meta decks of the format as of MSH.
2. Go to the Modern weekly tournaments at the LGS in Toronto, and ask for tips, borrow decks, and get a sense of playing in paper
3. Decide on a deck that I want to play, and hopefully find a local lender who will let me pilot it for the RC
4. Grind a ton of matches on the chosen deck both in paper and on MTGO.
5. Prepare side-board lists for the decks I'm likely to play against, and look out for meta changes based on the new sets coming out.

The constraints are what make this an exciting experience for me. I'm fairly busy with work and even with Melee, my main game, I rarely play outside of the weekly tournaments anymore. So with that I want to track both financial costs and time that I'm spending learning Modern, to see what I'm realistically putting into the project. So far the breakdown is:

Financial Costs (in CAD):
- 100 dollars at the print shop. I printed 13 decks, so around 112 pages at 0.75 each, and they cut it for me for 5 dollars which is a huge time save.
- 50 for 1100 sleeves. I got the cheapest sleeves I could find that were perfect fit, which were at 3.99 + tax for 100.

Time Costs:
- 1 hour - I watched this video (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pN7LsoCdKIw) and wrote down all of the decks I want to proxy.
- 4 hours - I found a netdeck for each on mtg goldfish, and copied all their exports into mtg print to generate the print files for me.
- 4 hours - A shocker to me, but sleeving this many cards, especially since I'm sliding them in to cheap sleeves with bulk is taking me quite a while.
- 1 hour - ~ writing this post ~ 

I'm posting this on reddit with the hopes that folks with a lot of experience in Modern can give me tips and help me adjust my plan, or tell me if anything seems too ambitious. I don't have any illusions that I would qualify for the pro tour, but even winning a few games at the RC would make me happy.
